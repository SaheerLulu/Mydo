# LinkedIn Post 1 - Pharmacy Retail (the expiry problem)

---

We sat with a pharmacy owner a few weeks ago who opened a drawer behind the counter and showed us a stack of boxes, most of them six to eight weeks from expiry. He had a rough sense of what was in there. What he didn't have was the year's total, which came to about the price of a decent used car.

That's the quiet thing about expiry losses. They never land as one painful number. It's a few hundred here, a tray written off there, and the business only really feels it when the accountant asks why shrinkage crept up again.

It was never carelessness on his part. Nobody can hold thousands of batch dates across three or four branches in their head while also serving a queue.

This is the boring, unglamorous work that software is genuinely good at. Medloop tracks every pack by batch and expiry, flags what's about to turn weeks ahead of time, and (the part owners tend to like) points out which branch is short of that exact item, so the stock can move before it dies on a shelf.

The pharmacies running it this way tend to knock write-offs down by roughly a fifth to a third. It isn't magic. It's just finally seeing the leak while there's still time to do something about it.

We wrote up how it actually works, with the screens, here: https://saheerlulu.github.io/mydo/blogs/pharmacy-retail.html
